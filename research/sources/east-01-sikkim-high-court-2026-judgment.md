## J U D G M E N T (ORAL)

#### (A. Muhamed Mustaque, C.J.)

This litigation is partly contributed to by the Petitioner, Mr. 
Bikash Bhattarai and partly by the Sikkim Public Service Commission 
(SPSC).

2. The Petitioner claimed that he belongs to the OBC Central List 
(CL), and applied for the post of Horticulture Development Officer 
(HDO), bearing Rank No.7. There were a total 15 vacancies for the 
said post. At the time of scrutiny, instead of producing a community 
certificate showing that he belongs to the OBC (CL), he produced an 
OBC State List (SL) certificate. The SPSC, by overlooking his 
application, accepted that community certificate and appointed him 
against the quota reserved for the OBC State List. The learned 
Senior Counsel appearing for the Petitioner submits that it was 
based on the advice given by the authorities concerned in this 
regard. Apparently, this Court cannot accept the said argument. 
Once an application is submitted and the cut-off date has expired,

3. Respondent No.10 in Writ Petition No.16 of 2026, who is a writ 
petitioner in WP(C) No.25 of 2025, appears to have lodged a 
complaint before the State Scrutiny Committee. The Scrutiny 
Committee became oblivious to its role in the matter. The mandate 
of the Scrutiny Committee is to determine the caste status of the 
person concerned. Instead of embarking upon an inquiry into the 
caste status, it embarked upon an inquiry into the manner in which 
the certificate was obtained by the Petitioner. No inquiry was 
conducted regarding the caste status of the Petitioner in WP(C) 
No.16 of 2026. On this ground alone, the order passed by the 
Scrutiny Committee is liable to be set aside.

4. The Scrutiny Committee should be reminded of its role. Its 
role is to determine the caste status of the person concerned and 
make a recommendation to the appropriate authority regarding such 
caste status. It is on the basis of the report/recommendation of the 
Scrutiny Committee that the employer or the SPSC may act.

5. The Scrutiny Committee has no responsibility to conduct an 
inquiry into the fraudulent manner in which the certificate was 
produced before the SPSC. It is for the SPSC to act upon the said 
report/recommendation and take an appropriate decision 
accordingly. However, since there was no determination regarding 
the caste status of the Petitioner, the decision of the Scrutiny 
Committee dated 16.10.2025 in WP(C) No.16 of 2026 is liable to be 
set aside and is accordingly set aside.

6. The next question is whether the Petitioner can claim 
reservation under the OBC (SL). Whether he belongs to the OBC 
(SL) or the OBC (CL) is a contentious issue. This Court cannot 
decide that issue at this stage. However, as is evident from the 
pleading and the documents produced before this Court by the 
Sikkim Public Service Commission, the Petitioner had applied under 
the OBC (CL). This means that he cannot claim reservation under 
the OBC (SL). If he belongs to the OBC (SL), it is open to him to 
apply under the OBC (SL) pursuant to any future notification.

7. Then comes the core issue, i.e. whether the termination of the 
Petitioner is justified or not. The Petitioner was appointed under the 
OBC (SL), which is reserved exclusively for candidates belonging to 
the OBC (SL). He cannot claim appointment since he had not applied 
under the OBC (SL). Therefore, the termination order dated 
06.03.2026 is justified.

8. If Respondent No.10, Avinandan Sharma, is the appropriate 
candidate as per the rank list published by the SPSC, the SPSC is at 
liberty to appoint Respondent No.10.

9. If that be the case, the writ petition filed by Respondent No.10 
i.e. WP(C) No.25 of 2025 (*Avinandan Sharma vs. State of Sikkim 
and Ors.),* has to be allowed. His appointment in place of the writ 
petitioner in WP(C) No.16 of 2026 *(Shri Bikash Bhattarai vs. The 
Government of Sikkim, Through the Chief Secretary and Ors.)* shall 
be considered.

10.  This Court understands that there is a possibility that the 
Petitioner could stake claim under the general merit quota based on 
his rank. It is submitted by the learned Additional Advocate General

Page **6** of **7** that the Petitioner will be considered for any future vacancy likely to 
arise.

11. It is to be noted that, in such circumstances, it would be 
appropriate to pass the following order:

(i) The State shall consider accommodating the Petitioner against 
any future vacancy likely to arise. The same shall be done within 
two months.

(ii) If no future vacancy arises within two months, the petitioner 
shall be appointed by creating a supernumerary post, and the said 
post shall be regularised against the next available substantive 
vacancy that may arise in future.

12. This is in order to protect those who are already in service and 
to avoid further litigation in the matter.

13. Accordingly, **_WP(C) No.16 of 2026_** and **_WP(C) No.25 of 
2025,_** stands disposed of on above terms.
