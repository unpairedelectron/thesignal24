3. Brief fact of this case are that pursuant to the
advertisement, issued by the Bihar Public Service Commission
(hereinafter referred to as BPSC) on 06/02/2020 bearing No
1/2020, writ  petitioner (appellant herein) filed an application.
Thereafter, he took part in the examination held 07/02/2021
through an admit card issued by BPSC, as Scheduled Caste
Category Candidate. Being the qualified candidate in preliminary
examination, petitioner was allotted admit card to appear in Mains
Examination scheduled to be held on and from 12/11/2022 to
15/11/2022 and after passing the Mains Examination, she appeared
in the interview with all documents but when the BPSC published
the final result of Assistant Prosecution Officer on 2.12.2023,
petitioner was amongst the eight candidates declared unsuccessful
on the sole ground that her caste/domicile certificate was not
issued based on her father's permanent place of residence. Her
father is a permanent resident of the State of Uttar Pradesh,
whereas she got married in 1996 in Biharand since then she has
resided in Bihar continuously. Aggrieved appellant filed C.W.J.C.
No, 1264 of 2024, wherein this Court vide order dated 08.02.2024
granted her liberty to approach the Commission for redressal of
her grievance. The representation submitted by the appellant was
rejected vide Memo No. 5018 dated 07.03.2024, and her review
application dated 18.03.2024 was also turned down vide letter
dated  26.04.20248.  Challenging  these  rejection  orders,  the
appellant filed C.W.J.C. No. 11672 of 2024, which was dismissed
by the Learned Single Judge on 06.08.2024. Hence, this Letters
Patent Appeal.

---

4. Learned counsel for the  appellant submits that the
appellant belongs to the Dushadh caste, which is recognized as a
Scheduled Caste in both the State of Uttar Pradesh (her natal State)
and the State of Bihar (her marital State). She completed her
education in Bihar after her marriage on 27.02.1996 and has been
permanently  domiciled  in  Bihar  for  over  28  years.
Under Sections 15 and 16 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925,
upon marriage, a woman acquires the domicile of her husband.
Thus, her domicile is unquestionably Bihar.

5. Per contra, learned counsel appearing for the State
and the Commission support the impugned judgment and submit
that the conditions governing reservation were clearly stipulated in
Advertisement No. 01/2020 before commencement of the selection
process. It is submitted that Column 6(ii) of the advertisement
specifically provided that the benefit of reservation on the basis of
caste would be available only to candidates who are permanent
residents/original residents of Bihar. Candidates residing outside
Bihar  were  not  entitled  to  claim  such  reservation.
It is further submitted that Column 6(iii) (A) required candidates
belonging to the Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe categories
to  produce  both  caste  and  permanent  residence/domicile
certificates issued by the competent authority. Learned counsel for
the Commission further relies upon the instructions contained in
the  interview  letter,  particularly  Clause  5(ii),  read  with  the
Government  instructions  dated  08.03.2011  and  10.03.2011,
requiring married women claiming reservation to furnish the
relevant certificates in the name and address of their father and not
their  husband.  It  is  contended  that  the  appellant's  father  is
admittedly a resident of Uttar Pradesh and, therefore, the appellant
cannot claim the benefit of Bihar-specific reservation merely on the basis of her marriage or subsequent  residence in Bihar.
It is thus submitted that the Commission has acted strictly in
accordance with the applicable policy and the conditions of the
advertisement, and no interference with the judgment of the
learned Single Judge is warranted.

6. Learned counsel for the Commission further contends
that in the process of advertisement, it has been clearly mentioned
on the line of reservation that those candidates who are not
resident of Bihar, shall not be granted reservation and as for grant
of  reservation  permanent  resident/domicile  certificate  is  the
necessary ingredients. Not only in the advertisement, rather in the
interview letter also, it has been categorically mentioned that the
resident certificate must be in the name of candidate’s father and
not in the name of candidate’s husband.  Secondly, ‘the rules of the
game’ were laid down prior to the examination process and having
participated with full knowledge of these terms, the candidate
cannot challenge them post-selection.

7. We have heard learned counsel for the parties and
perused the materials available on the record.

8. The argument of learned counsel for the  appellant
based on Sections 15 and 16 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 is
wholly misplaced. Civil domicile acquired by operation of law
upon  marriage  does  not  automatically  confer  constitutional
reservation benefits under Article 16(4) of the Constitution of
India. The fundamental objective behind restricting cross-state
reservation benefits is to prevent candidates belonging to general
or forward classes in their home State from claiming unearned
benefits in another State by marriage or relocation. A person who
is a member of a Scheduled Caste in State 'A' cannot carry that

WEB COPY status to claim reservation benefits in State 'B' upon voluntary
migration or marriage. Reservation rights are anchored to the
origin  and  birth  of  the  candidate  in  the  state  where  the
disadvantage was historically suffered. In this regard, reliance is
placed on the decision of the Constitution Benches of the Hon'ble
Supreme  Court  in  **Action  Committee  on  Issue  of  Caste
Certificate to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the**
**State of Maharashtra v. Union of India,** reported in **(1994) 5
SCC 244** and **Bir Singh v. Delhi Jal Board,** reported in **(2018) 10
SCC 312.**

9. In the present case, petitioner is not entitled for
benefit as the BPSC has framed the Rule in the light of the State
policy and prior to advertisement, this matter has been decided at
the  Government level,  which  has  also  got  reflected  in
Advertisement Columns 6 (ii) and (iii)(A) as well as in clause 5(ii)
of the interview letter and, hence, no case of the petitioner is made
out.

10. It is also undisputed that ‘rules of the game’ has
already been fixed prior to entering the examination process and,
therefore, the decision taken by the BPSC is completely in
accordance  with  rule  of  which  the  petitioner  already  had
knowledge. Upon perusal of the provisions of law, it transpires that
prior to entering into the advertisement, rule was framed and since
rule was framed then in that view of the matter the petitioner has
no option but to follow the rule until and unless it is changed.
Terms  of  advertisement  issued  in  pursuant  to  any  rules  or
notification,  binds  parties  having  trapping  of  statutory
prescriptions, unless it becomes contrary to either a rule or an Act.
Reliance is placed on the decision of the Division bench of this

11. We, therefore, find no perversity or illegality in the
conclusion reached by the learned Single Judge. The appellant has
failed to demonstrate that the Commission acted contrary to the
applicable recruitment conditions or constitutional provisions.

12. In the result, this Letters Patent Appeal stands
dismissed.
