---
name: Reservation archive editorial rule
description: Product and editorial constraints for the India-wide caste-based reservation archive.
---

The reservation archive should separate reporting, legal/policy records, historical documents, paper notes, explainers, and ground reporting. Every record should make its source, publication date, region/state, and verification status visible, and starter content must not be presented as breaking news.

Published-but-unverified records may remain discoverable in archive, timeline, map, and recent-record surfaces when their status is explicit. They must not be hero- or featured-promoted; those placements require a non-unverified status and a direct source link.

**Why:** Caste and reservation are sensitive, high-stakes topics where decontextualized claims can inflame rather than inform. Discovery is not endorsement: hiding a published record weakens the archive, while hero placement signals stronger editorial confidence.

**How to apply:** Keep explicit status labels in broad discovery surfaces, but require both a non-unverified status and a direct source link for homepage hero or featured-investigation placement. In compact homepage headline groups, summarize statuses once below the headlines rather than prefixing every link; on article cards and detail pages, place the story-specific status below its title. Preserve calm, precise language; prefer primary documents and reputable reporting; keep current reporting distinct from historical material; and add ingestion/moderation before claiming comprehensive live coverage.
